How to Prepare for Moving Day: 10 Things People Forget

5 August 2026 Boxit Logistics Team

Moving house is exciting, but it's also one of life's biggest logistical challenges. Between packing boxes, updating your address and organising the paperwork, it's surprisingly easy to overlook small details that can make moving day far more stressful than it needs to be.

The good news is that a little preparation goes a long way.

Whether you're moving across town or relocating further afield, planning ahead can help avoid delays, reduce stress and make the day run much more smoothly.

Here are ten things people often forget until it's too late.

1. Confirm Everything With Your Removal Company

It might sound obvious, but don't assume everything is already arranged.

A few days before your move, confirm:

  • Your booking date and arrival time.
  • The collection and delivery addresses.
  • Parking arrangements.
  • Any access restrictions.
  • Whether large furniture needs dismantling.

A quick phone call can prevent unnecessary confusion on moving day.

2. Pack an Essentials Box You'll Actually Need

One of the biggest mistakes people make is packing absolutely everything.

When you arrive at your new home, the last thing you want is to search through twenty boxes looking for your phone charger or kettle.

Keep one box or overnight bag with essentials such as:

Phone chargers Medication Important documents Tea, coffee and snacks Toilet roll Basic toiletries A change of clothes Children's or pets' essentials if needed

Think of it as your "first night" box.

You'll thank yourself later.

3. Measure Doorways Before Moving Large Furniture

Many people measure the furniture but forget to measure the route it needs to take.

Wardrobes, sofas and bed frames often become stuck because of:

Narrow hallways Tight corners Low ceilings Small front doors Steep staircases

Checking access in advance gives you time to decide whether furniture needs dismantling before moving day.

4. Defrost Your Fridge and Freezer Early

This is one of the most commonly forgotten jobs.

Leaving it until moving morning usually means dealing with leaking water while you're trying to load the van.

If possible:

  • Empty the freezer.
  • Defrost it 24 hours beforehand.
  • Dry it thoroughly.
  • Leave the doors slightly open until transport.

It saves time and helps prevent unpleasant smells developing during the move.

5. Label Boxes by Room, Not Just What's Inside

Writing "Kitchen" on a box is much more useful than writing "Plates".

Even better, include both.

For example:

  • Kitchen - Glasses
  • Bedroom - Winter Clothes
  • Office - Computer Equipment

Clear labels help everyone know exactly where each box belongs, making unpacking much quicker.

6. Keep Important Documents With You

Passports. Driving licences. Mortgage paperwork. Tenancy agreements. Completion documents.

These shouldn't disappear into a removal box.

Keep valuable paperwork in a clearly labelled folder that stays with you throughout the move.

The same applies to jewellery, cash and sentimental items.

7. Don't Forget Utilities and Address Changes

Changing your address is easy to forget when you're focused on packing.

Before moving, remember to notify:

Your energy supplier Water provider Broadband company Bank GP surgery DVLA Insurance providers

You may also want to set up a Royal Mail redirection service so important post reaches your new address while everything is being updated.

8. Make Sure There's Somewhere for the Removal Van to Park

Parking is something many people don't think about until the removal van arrives.

If the crew has to park a long distance away, loading can take significantly longer.

If possible:

  • Reserve a parking space.
  • Inform neighbours if access may be affected.
  • Check whether permits are required.
  • Let your removal company know about any restrictions in advance.

Small details like this can make a big difference on the day.

9. Do One Final Walk Around Before You Leave

When the house looks empty, it's tempting to think everything has been packed.

Take five extra minutes.

Open every cupboard. Look inside wardrobes. Check lofts, garages, sheds and under the stairs.

Many forgotten belongings are only discovered after the keys have been handed over.

10. Remember That You Don't Have to Do Everything Yourself

Moving house is hard work.

If you're trying to pack, dismantle furniture, load a van and clean the property all in one day, it's easy to become overwhelmed.

Professional movers can often help with much more than simply transporting boxes.

Depending on the service you choose, they may also assist with furniture dismantling and reassembly, careful furniture delivery, and even short-term storage if your new property isn't quite ready.

Getting the right support can make moving day far less stressful and help everything run more efficiently.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I start preparing for moving day?

Ideally, start planning several weeks in advance. This gives you plenty of time to organise packing, update your address and arrange any services you need.

What should I pack last?

Keep everyday essentials, important documents, medication, chargers and a few basic household items with you until the day of the move.

What should I do the night before moving?

Finish packing, charge your phone, double-check your essentials box, confirm arrangements with your removal company and make sure large appliances such as your fridge freezer are ready for transport.

Should I dismantle furniture before the removal van arrives?

If you know large furniture won't fit through doors or staircases, dismantling it beforehand can save time. Alternatively, some removal companies offer furniture dismantling and reassembly as part of their service.

A Little Preparation Makes Moving Day Much Easier

No move is completely stress-free, but good preparation can make a huge difference.

By planning ahead and avoiding these common oversights, you'll give yourself the best chance of a smoother, more organised moving day.
